I have been inside this hustle culture since my school days. Back then I used to consume a lot of YouTube self-help content. Read a lot of self-help books too.

And honestly, self-help is like a cheap drug.

The more you consume it, the more you crave it. It gives you the illusion of progress. You feel like you are changing your life while sitting in the same fucking room doing nothing.

A lot of it is just mental masturbation.

And slowly, without realizing it, the internet starts feeding you dopamine for ruining yourself.

That’s the dangerous part.

## Exhaustion Became A Personality Trait

The internet slowly converted exhaustion from a body signal into a flex.

The more dark circles you have, the more “dedicated” you look.

The more coffee you drink, the more “locked in” you look.

The more sleep you sacrifice, the more serious people think you are about life.

People started glorifying:

-   4 AM screenshots
-   Packed schedules
-   Caffeine addiction
-   Burnout posts
-   “No days off”
-   Sleeping 3 hours
-   Working 18 hours

Burnout got disguised as ambition.

Fatigue became aesthetic.

And honestly, tiredness itself became people’s personality trait.

People started competing in suffering.

Everybody wanted to look like the hardest worker in the room.

But little did they know:

The more you push your brain like that, the more you slowly fry it on a fucking stove running at 5000°C.

And social media amplifies all of this.

Someone posts:

> “Slept 3 hours. Worked 18 hours. Grinded nonstop.”

And suddenly everybody treats them like they are serious about life.

Meanwhile someone doing a normal job, sleeping properly, spending time with family, chilling for one evening?

The internet acts like that person is trapped inside some “matrix”.

## I Have Been On Both Sides

And the reason I can talk about this properly is because I have lived on both sides.

I have been the guy who admired:

-   Dark circles
-   Packed schedules
-   Caffeine abuse
-   Late-night work sessions
-   Sleeping on the desk
-   Nonstop grinding

I genuinely used to think that was greatness.

Most of my college life went into this.

And my college itself was already exhausting.

The timings were usually:

-   8 to 3
-   Or 10 to 5

And travel alone used to take around 2 hours daily.

Add getting ready and all the random bullshit, and almost half my day was already gone.

So whatever time I had left, I forced myself to grind.

Every single night.

The moment I came back home, I would sit down and immediately start working.

Courses.  
Coding.  
Building.  
Learning.  
Anything.

And one thing about me was that I had consumed so much self-help content that I stopped questioning myself entirely.

I never asked:

> “Why am I even doing this?”

I just executed. I became a machine.

Because every self-help book tells you:

> “The more you do, the more better you become.”

And yes, there is truth in that.

I’m not shitting on hard work.

But I took that idea too fucking far.

## My Brain Never Learned How To End A Day

That’s the scariest part.

My brain genuinely forgot how to end a day.

For normal people, a day ends when night comes.

For me, the day ended only when the goal was completed.

And the goals themselves were:

-   Monthly
-   Yearly
-   Long-term

So technically my brain never stopped running.

There was no emotional shutdown.

No closure.

No “okay, enough for today”.

Only:

> “Do more.”

A normal day for me looked like this:

-   Sleep for 2–4 hours
-   Wake up barely 30 minutes before college
-   Rush everything
-   Survive the entire day like a zombie
-   Drink 3–4 coffees
-   Drink Red Bull/Monster or some caffeine-loaded garbage
-   Come back home
-   Start grinding again
-   Eventually pass out on the desk

And yes, I literally used to sleep on my table every alternate day instead of my bed.

My brain was so fried that life stopped feeling segmented.

You know that feeling when you wake up and instantly continue the same thought process from before sleeping?

That was my life continuously.

There was no reset.

Only continuation.

My philosophy genuinely became:

> “If a day has 24 hours, I should be productive for 23.59 of them.”

That’s how deep I was into hustle culture.

## Being Busy Became Status

Nowadays people flex being busy the same way people flex money.

You will notice how proudly people say:

-   “I barely sleep.”
-   “I haven’t taken a break in months.”
-   “I’m insanely busy.”

As if resting makes you unsuccessful.

People started treating rest like unemployment.

And the internet constantly makes you feel behind.

Someone is:

-   Launching a startup
-   Coding
-   Building an audience
-   Making money at 20
-   Going viral
-   Getting fit
-   Networking

And suddenly taking one evening off feels illegal.

Even if you did 99 things right that day, your brain will only focus on the 1 thing you didn’t optimize.

Because somewhere another guy is grinding harder.

That pressure slowly becomes internal.

And then even when nobody is forcing you anymore, your own brain keeps whipping you.

## Hustle Culture Removed The Finish Line

The problem is not ambition.

Hard work matters.  
Discipline matters.  
Grinding matters too if you genuinely need it.

I still believe that.

But hustle culture removed the finish line.

There is always:

-   More money
-   More growth
-   More followers
-   More optimization
-   More networking
-   More learning

People are running infinite races with invisible finish lines.

And your brain adapts to that.

Your brain is neuroplastic.  
It reshapes itself based on repetition.

So after years of overworking, I trained my brain to permanently stay in overdrive mode.

Now even when I try living healthy:

-   Sleeping on time
-   Waking up on time
-   Working normally
-   Hitting gym
-   Relaxing

…I can barely sustain it for a month before my brain starts dragging me back into chaos.

Random all-nighters.  
Random overworking.  
Random guilt.

Even while writing this blog right now at night, my brain is still telling me:

> “Learn something.”  
> “Build something.”  
> “Test some new agent.”  
> “Do more.”

That’s the scary part.

Even rest starts feeling uncomfortable.

## The Body Eventually Collects The Debt

You can survive on 2–3 hours sleep for some time.

But eventually your body collects the debt with interest.

In college, my pattern became very obvious.

One semester:

> Insane grind

Next semester:

> Completely dead inside

-   I would sleep the whole day.
-   Brain fog.
-   Zero attention span.
-   No energy.

Then repeat the cycle again next year.

Finally during third year, I reduced my grind massively.

From around 12+ hours daily to maybe 4 focused hours.

And honestly?  
That alone improved my health a lot.

## My Health Was Absolutely Fucked

A few months ago I got blood tests done.

The results shocked me.

My Vitamin D was almost dead.

B 12 low.  
Magnesium low.  
Multiple deficiencies.

And mentally?

My brain genuinely feels fried.

Constant thinking.  
Constant planning.  
Constant cognitive overload.

If normal human cognitive usage is 100%, I think I trained my brain to operate at 150% for years.

So now even when I work at normal levels, my brain feels unsatisfied.

It only feels calm when completely exhausted.

That is terrifying if you think deeply about it.

I literally trained myself to sleep only after mentally destroying myself.

## Burnout Leaks Slowly Into Personality

The dangerous thing about burnout is that it doesn’t arrive dramatically.

It leaks slowly into your personality.

You become:

-   Emotionally numb
-   Irritated
-   Aggressive
-   Detached
-   Unable to enjoy rest

Even my friends told me multiple times:

> “Bro, day by day you are becoming dull.”

And honestly they were right.

You stop feeling alive.  
You keep functioning, but you stop feeling alive.

## Performative Hard Work Is Everywhere

Social media also created performative hard work.

A lot of people love _looking hardworking_ more than actually doing meaningful work.

Some people literally document burnout like character development.

Like bro:

> You are not becoming the main character

You are just ruining your nervous system.

And I know this because I have suffered from it myself.

Not completely destroyed thankfully.

But enough to understand where this road leads.

Burnout is not some cool aesthetic.

It’s horrible.

## Sustainable People Look Boring But They Survive

Nowadays I relate more with sustainable people.

They may look boring.

But they have:

-   Proper sleep
-   Consistency
-   Balance
-   Stable routines
-   Healthy energy

Hustle culture looks cinematic.

Sustainable growth looks boring.

But boring survives longer.

If you sprint at marathon pace, you won’t even finish the marathon.

Speed is not the real problem.

Pace is.

## Your Body Is Alive, Not A Tool

Our body is alive.

You cannot treat it like a tool.

You should care about your body more than your gadgets.

Sleep properly.  
Eat properly.  
Train properly.

Because what’s the point of luxury if the person enjoying it is already dead inside?

An unsustainable grind eventually leaves behind only two things:

-   Money
-   A dead man

And there is no point of luxury then.

## Where I Am Right Now

Right now I’m trying to shift towards sustainable grind.

Trying to:

-   Sleep 7–8 hours
-   Hit gym regularly
-   Eat clean
-   Stop ruining my bedtime
-   Create a more predictable life

Not because I lost ambition.

But because I want to survive long enough to actually enjoy whatever I build.

I still haven’t fully figured it out.

Honestly, I don’t even know if I fully can.

But at least now I understand the problem.

And I think that matters.

## Final Thought

People chase freedom while destroying their ability to enjoy it.

But at the same time…

The longer you delay your dreams, the lesser time you may have later to enjoy them.

And honestly, that contradiction is exactly where I’m stuck right now too.
